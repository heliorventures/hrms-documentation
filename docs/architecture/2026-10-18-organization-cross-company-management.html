How the implemented services fit together

Login once: an HR employee in Company A opens /organization-workspace. Current source Auth verifies the real account. Explicit organization roles determine which companies and actions appear. A local HR role alone remains company-local; organization administration, delegation, payroll, reporting and history are independent capabilities.
  1. Connect and activate the company

    The source company administrator accepts an invitation. Organization HR sends a versioned membership installation. The fixed source confirms its fence; activation is visible only after its authenticated ACK.

    Metadata: desired revision → source: installed fence
  2. Synchronize identity and reporting lines

    Bounded directory pages carry stable employee/user IDs and legacy reporting managers. Complete baselines are required before validating the combined people graph. No email/name matching creates identities.

    Directory refs + sync cursor + graph revision
  3. Assign and install authority

    HR selects an employee and manager from either company. The primary graph must remain acyclic across all employments and reserved installations. Company B installs its assignment and returns an exact ACK. Role grants have separate company ACKs.

    Source assignment + capabilities + actor references
  4. Show work and execute safely

    Committed events populate the shared manager inbox. The manager opens live details from the source, then approves with its exact resource version and workflow step. The source repeats current policy, locks its fence and business rows, applies normal business rules and commits once.

    Business transaction + minimal receipt + canonical event
  5. Recover interrupted responses

    A stable command ID and source receipt resolve uncertain outcomes. After reload the workspace restores status-only pending references; private request bodies stay in memory. A broker delivery acknowledgement never means business approval. If a request never executed, an explicit cancel action checks the source under the original command lock and saves a compact cancellation receipt. Late delivery then cannot apply; an already committed result is returned unchanged.

    SUBMITTED → AWAITING_CONFIRMATION → source-confirmed result
  6. Read owner reporting with coverage

    Reporting and history require separate scoped grants. Accepted membership-specific capture, partition coverage and freshness qualify totals. Missing or stale capture appears as incomplete/unavailable; it never becomes a fabricated zero.

    ClickHouse versioned facts + PostgreSQL coverage metadata

History survives delivery delay and company changes

Company facts and organization configuration events have separate authenticated capture paths. Canonical company facts committed before suspension or retirement may arrive later. A known historical membership permits protected history retention; current projections and application access remain disabled. Unknown or never-accepted organization/company claims are quarantined. Rejoining or resuming requires new capture acceptance before completeness is asserted. Configuration activity has its own ClickHouse control history; global identity/security records stay operator-only. The application exposes observed configuration activity without claiming complete coverage.

Diagnostic collection reads one explicitly mounted, read-only service log file per collector instance. It parses the Docker wrapper and Rust JSON, drops free-form messages and nested attributes, and forwards fixed diagnostic codes/severity with deployment-bound company/service attribution. No Docker socket, whole-host log access, browser tokens or private request bodies are required.

Company and manager changes

Manager replacement: change the existing assignment to a new revision, validate desired plus still-installed graphs, and install atomically at the source. The old source snapshot remains reserved until ACK. Suspension: central access stops immediately; source installation drains in-flight operations and pauses capture/bootstrap while preserving reporting ownership. Resume: install the new ACTIVE membership revision, synchronize directory, and explicitly reinstall business grants. Detach: first reassign/end and install incoming reports in other companies; then install terminal retirement. Source ACK finalizes DETACHED. Current local primary reporting is restored to a real local manager; current foreign primary becomes unassigned for local HR to configure.

Release gates: this document does not authorize deployment or deletion. Production TLS/ACL setup, upgraded-company migration acceptance, complete historical reconciliation, backup/restore drills and live browser acceptance must be recorded independently. Keep business decisions, compact receipts and temporary unconfirmed outbox rows. Legacy audit/notification outboxes are removed only after every real consumer and retained-history equivalent is proven. Current Chrome, Edge and IAB browser surfaces were unavailable; the new screens have not passed live browser acceptance.

  1. Business decision or administration

    The owning service validates exact current authority, expected source version, entitlements and domain rules. It commits the business change, compact receipt and whitelisted event atomically. Credential-bearing payloads never enter intent/history/journal storage.

  2. Independent delivery

    CDC publishes committed envelopes. Inbox commits its projection independently of ClickHouse. History and reporting each acknowledge their own store; replay keeps original IDs and detects conflicting versions.

  3. Payroll execution after acceptance

    The source persists a bounded immutable job, not a reusable token. The worker claims a lease, revalidates original account/principal/membership/grant epochs, recalculates source rules and commits business effects plus terminal state once. Expired leases cannot commit.

  4. History export and cleanup

    Source-owned resumable chunks preserve genuine IDs, times, hashes and provenance. AES-GCM archives verify immutable range/count/hash manifests. Cleanup defaults to dry-run and requires accepted fixed-source policy, retained history, restore proof, cutover and exact locked candidate hashes. Business/audit/decision receipts are never cleanup candidates.

Operational history is retained; atomic delivery state remains small. Kafka/ClickHouse do not remove the need for source business records, minimal command/job receipts and a transactional outbox. Outboxes are bounded publish buffers and are cleaned only after proof. Unsupported legacy labels are explicitly UNMAPPED; earlier unavailable history is UNKNOWN. Private original audit snapshots need the protected source backup/restore path.

03

Architecture: company transactions, shared events and retained history

Recommended baseline: separate company PostgreSQL databases; a small transactional outbox captured by Debezium CDC; Kafka for event delivery; shared PostgreSQL for organization configuration and inbox state; ClickHouse for reporting and searchable history. Diagnostic logs arrive through OpenTelemetry. These are proposed deployment components, not infrastructure already installed.

1 · Decide locally

Company owns the result

SBL validates and saves SBL leave, timesheets and payroll. SCL does the same for its employees. Each runtime can access only its own database.

2 · Publish once logically

One business-event contract

The transaction records its structured event in a temporary outbox. CDC transfers that committed record to Kafka. Consumers reuse it for inbox, reporting and history.

3 · Read permitted views

One application workspace

Managers see My Approvals and My Team. Shared HR sees Organization Management. Owners see the Organization Dashboard. All views apply current permissions.

One business record, several consumers: the application defines and commits the event once. Kafka and ClickHouse retain copies for delivery and history. An inbox consumer stores current work; a history sink retains the timeline. The same event supplies both, avoiding independent application writes of identical long-term audit data.

Connections, ports and operating cost

A separate database does not inherently require a separate listening port. Capacity planning concerns connections and replication slots across runtime replicas and company pools. Reuse bounded pools, keep idle connections low, and budget CDC connections per database. CDC removes application outbox polling, but adds connector operations and transaction-log retention risk. Tenant-bound execution is a security requirement; it does not require one microservice per screen.

Managed Kafka, CDC and ClickHouse can reduce infrastructure administration. The application still owns authorization, idempotency, schema evolution, reconciliation and permanently failed work. Size retention and capacity from measured throughput, payload size and recovery objectives; moving logs alone does not establish lower cost.

Kafka is the proposed baseline for retained streams and independent consumers. RabbitMQ remains an alternative if delivery-focused workloads and hosting constraints favor it; introduce both only for a concrete need. CDC hosting, supported PostgreSQL topology, failover and connector permissions require implementation review.

A practical VPS deployment path

For a pilot, use a Linux VPS with Docker Engine and Docker Compose, persistent local SSD storage, private container networking and explicit resource limits. Keep the existing company databases in their current separate deployment. A dedicated infrastructure VPS helps isolate Kafka/ClickHouse disk and memory pressure from the application and transactional databases.

Single VPS means a single failure domain: multiple containers on one host do not provide host-level high availability. This layout is suitable for a pilot or an explicitly accepted downtime profile. A host/disk failure can stop delivery, history search and reporting together; the capture, retention and recovery policies must cover that outage.

Deployment sequence for a later infrastructure change

  1. Measure and choose the availability target

    Record VPS CPU/RAM, SSD capacity/IOPS, existing workloads, tenant count, peak event/log volume, payload sizes and retention. Estimate storage from bytes per day × retention × replication, plus working space. Benchmark before selecting final sizes.

  2. Prepare storage and private access

    Use explicit mounts such as application-folder shared/storage/kafka, shared/storage/clickhouse and collector buffer directories. Store configuration separately and secrets outside version control. Restrict ownership, configure log rotation and back up off-host.

  3. Install pinned infrastructure images

    Use reviewed versions/digests of the official apache/kafka and clickhouse/clickhouse-server images. Add a compatible Kafka Connect/Debezium runtime, history/reporting consumers and OpenTelemetry Collector. An image tag of latest is not a production version policy.

  4. Configure networking and durability

    Advertise Kafka addresses reachable by private clients; a generic HTTPS reverse proxy does not replace Kafka listener configuration. Keep Kafka, Connect administration, ClickHouse and replication endpoints off the public Internet. Configure TLS/authentication, topic permissions, persistent data/metadata paths and backup identities.

  5. Configure capture and destinations

    Enable supported PostgreSQL logical replication and restricted connector credentials. Create scoped routes, schema mappings, consumer groups, retention and failed-message handling. Protect Kafka Connect internal offsets/config/status topics. Create ClickHouse datasets with separate readers and retention policies.

  6. Pilot before service retirement

    Validate delivery, restarts, duplicates, access isolation, retention limits, archive restore and committed source outcomes in a non-production pilot. Cut over only after the cleanup gates below are satisfied. This document does not deploy containers or run these checks.

Queued decision flow — the suggested asynchronous option

Two distinct statuses: the command can be applied or not applied. The leave request can be approved, rejected, cancelled or still pending. If an attempted approval cannot be applied because the request was cancelled, show that reason; do not label the leave itself “rejected.”

Source publishing transaction

Write the business change and its outbox event together. CDC captures the committed outbox insert and publishes its stable event ID to Kafka. Each consumer keeps its own checkpoint. Kafka acceptance, history persistence and business completion are separate milestones. Destination retries move to consumer infrastructure; legacy webhook delivery is reconciled during cutover.

Receiving transaction

Validate the message and current actor authority. In one local transaction, deduplicate, check expected resource version, apply the valid change, record the command receipt and write the outcome event. Commit the broker acknowledgement only after the database commit. A retry reads the recorded outcome.

Ordering and duplicates: key messages by owning tenant + aggregate. Use source versions to reject obsolete updates even when messages arrive late or out of order. There is no assumed global ordering between grants, requests and reports. A duplicate API retry and queued delivery must share the same command identity; do not dispatch a second business action after an ambiguous timeout.

Delivery operations: monitor unpublished outbox age, processing lag, invalid schemas, failed-message queues, expired commands and missing outcomes. A command timeout is not proof that the action failed; reconcile against the source receipt. Replay uses existing IDs and versions. An unresolved queued command remains visible for recovery.

Broker and application responsibilities

Kafka provides retained streams, offsets and consumer groups. Configure retry topics or a consumer framework for bounded backoff, failed-message handling and recovery; Kafka alone does not classify business errors or implement application retries. Retry transient failures with the same identity. Record permanent domain rejection as an outcome. Invalid schemas or conflicting identities enter a protected quarantine and make coverage incomplete.

Managed hosting reduces infrastructure administration. The implementation still needs alert ownership, recovery procedures, schema compatibility, capacity budgets and tested restoration. Configure independent consumer groups so inbox, reporting and history each process the event and advance their own offsets. Topic permissions separately control which data each identity may read.

Cleanup sequence and exit criteria

  1. Inventory writers, readers and running jobs

    Trace all services, scheduled tasks, migrations, exports and external destinations. Classify each record as business state, temporary handoff, retry metadata or retained history. Record ownership and cutover state per company/stream.

  3. Backfill and reconcile retained history

    Export legacy audit/event records with stable identities and coverage manifests. Verify duplicates, actor mapping, historical queries, access controls and restoration. Mark genuinely missing old history explicitly.

  4. Cut over publication and destination consumers

    Establish a source watermark and one publisher owner per stream. Drain/reconcile pending and failed deliveries, enable CDC and broker consumers, and migrate webhook/finance integrations with stable IDs. Shadow projections must have external side effects disabled.

  5. Retire code and apply data retention

    Stop old deployments and schedules first. Remove obsolete polling, DB log writes, retry loops, readers, dependencies, configuration and log-only connection pools. Apply bounded cleanup and later schema removal only when recovery evidence and rollout gates permit.

  6. Record the release outcome

    Confirm current requests, shared approvals, payroll, external integrations and history remain correct; document rollback limits after history migration. Remaining policy and deployment decisions

Agreed direction: separate company databases, source-owned decisions, configurable cross-company access, and one structured business history outside tenant databases. Kafka + CDC, ClickHouse and a bounded transactional outbox are implemented in the current source. The following items record initial policy defaults and choices requiring launch sign-off; optional extensions are not automatically first-release requirements. Production acceptance remains governed by the evidence and release gates below.

Who can link a company?

Proposed: an organization administrator initiates, with verified authority from the participating company. Company HR can edit reporting assignments only within delegated company scope.

One primary manager or several?

Proposed: one primary manager per employee at a time; additional functional managers/reviewers with separate capabilities. Indirect-report access is explicitly configured.

What happens to pending approvals?

Proposed: preserve the historical assignee and require an audited reassignment when authority ends. Display blocked work to designated HR instead of selecting an implicit fallback.

Who sees confidential information?

Proposed: target-company policies define profile fields, leave attachments, draft reviews, and feedback visibility. Payroll and sensitive identity fields require separate grants.

Can a company join multiple organizations?

Proposed initial invariant: one active organization per company. This keeps the ownership and isolation rules clear; multi-group affiliations would need a separate policy model.

Delegation and temporary cover?

Proposed: an explicit, time-limited delegate assignment with its own permitted actions and audit history. Confirm whether this belongs in the first release.

Which payroll actions can shared HR perform?

Proposed: grant supported payroll read/manage operations explicitly by company. Confirm approval/finalization, exports and any payment-related authority separately, using actual domain permissions.

How will shared business runtimes be hosted?

The approved shared layout gives each business process an explicit company allowlist with a stable restricted service login. Dedicated company CDC and private-event workers keep their own scoped credentials. Rehearse authenticated routing, negative access and connection limits before release.

Hosting and command experience?

Baseline: Kafka + CDC + ClickHouse. Confirm managed/self-hosted operation, expected volume, recovery objectives and cost before production enablement. Default short decisions to source APIs; enable queued mode where pending confirmation is acceptable.

Which owner metrics and freshness target?

Agree the available source datasets, logged/submitted/approved definitions, company/project attribution, historical coverage, detail permissions and acceptable delay. Show incomplete data while backfill or source delivery is pending.

History coverage and retention?

Define required business/security transitions, historical values and the start of guaranteed coverage. Set searchable, archive and diagnostic retention separately. Do not assume unlimited retention or reconstruct missing legacy actions from current state.

Safe cleanup and outage limits?

Specify source capture acknowledgements, archive coverage evidence and the grace window used for outbox cleanup. Set WAL/outbox limits, alert owners, backpressure behavior and recovery procedures. Do not enable age-only deletion.

ClickHouse consistency and restore?

Select fact-version keys, partitioning, duplicate handling and correction-safe aggregate queries. Choose backups/archive controls and prove restoration without triggering payroll or notifications.

Who can search or export history?

Define history-reader, security-reader and export capabilities separately from ordinary HR, manager and owner reporting roles. Protect sensitive fields, former memberships and company detachments.
